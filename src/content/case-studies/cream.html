How we helped CREAM stop losing customers to downtime and keep more of every sale

CREAM is a platform where African musicians and creators sell their work and fans win prizes for supporting them. The client

Who CREAM is

CREAM (Creative, Reality, Entertainment, Art, Music) describes itself as Africa's first African-owned, direct-to-consumer super-fan platform. It was founded by Afrobeats artist D'banj and reports over 7 million subscribers across Nigeria.

The business has two kinds of customer:

  • Creators (musicians, video makers, comedians) who upload their work, sell it and get paid.
  • Fans who stream, buy and download that work, and who take part in prize draws, celebrity auctions and talent contests.

Fans reach CREAM in three ways: the website at creamplatform.com, the rewards site, and by dialling *463# on any MTN phone.

Plain-language explainer

Why a business like CREAM needs USSD

USSD is the menu that appears when you dial a code like *463#. It looks old-fashioned, but it solves a problem a website cannot: it works on any phone, including basic phones, with no internet and no data bundle. In Nigeria and across Africa, that means millions of people who would never open a website or install an app can still buy from you in a few seconds.

The customer pays with airtime, which they already have. That convenience has a cost: the mobile network and the aggregator (the company that connects a business to the network) each take a share of the payment.

What we did

Nine changes, each tied to a business result

We use AI to write code faster, but the code is the easy part. The value is in understanding where a business is losing money or customers, deciding what to build, and checking that it works. Each change below starts from a business problem.

Stable servers that stay up

Problem
The platform went offline once or twice a week because the server could not manage its load.
What we built
We packaged the back end into containers, so each part runs in its own controlled space with the resources it needs. We replaced the old Apache web server with Nginx, which handles heavy traffic with far less strain. New versions now go live through an automatic process that checks the new version is healthy and switches back to the last good version on its own if it is not.
Result
No more weekly outages. An update now interrupts the site for under half a second, and a bad release can no longer take the site down.

One shared engine for every channel

Problem
The website, the business rules and the data were one tangled block. Every change risked breaking something else.
What we built
We separated the "front" (what customers see) from the "engine" (the rules about payments, draws, bids and payouts). The engine is now one central service, and the website, the rewards site, the USSD menu and the mobile app all use it.
Result
A rule changes in one place and every channel gets it at once. New products, like a new contest or payment method, launch in days instead of weeks.

One login for every CREAM app

Problem
CREAM runs at least four apps, including the main site, the rewards site and CreamPay. Each had its own login, so a fan signed in to one was a stranger to the others.
What we built
A single CREAM account system that every app trusts, built on OAuth and OpenID Connect, the same open standards behind "Sign in with Google". Sign in on any CREAM app and you are signed in on all of them, without seeing another login screen. Sign out on one and you are signed out everywhere, which matters on shared phones.
Result
Fans move between buying music, playing for prizes and managing their wallet without friction. CREAM sees one customer across every app, so a song bought on the main site can be matched to a draw entry on the rewards site, and new apps can join the same login in days.

A website that works on every phone and shows up on Google

Problem
The site broke on small screens, and song and creator pages could not be found on Google.
What we built
We rebuilt the website from scratch so it adapts to any screen. We then moved it to a set-up (Next.js) that prepares each page in full before it reaches the visitor, which is what search engines need in order to read it. Every song, video and creator page now has its own title, description and share preview, and a site map lists about 2,700 content pages for Google. The site is available in English, Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa and French.
Result
Fans can find CREAM's content through search, and links shared on WhatsApp and social media show a proper preview card. That brings in visitors without paid advertising.

A faster, safer USSD service

Problem
The *463# menu ran on loose PHP scripts that were hard to change and test. The song list took over 10 seconds to load, long enough for the session to time out and the sale to be lost.
What we built
We rebuilt the USSD service as one organised application that answers MTN exactly as before, so MTN did not have to change anything. It records every purchase before charging, never charges twice for the same request, and ignores duplicate payment confirmations. The song list now loads instantly from a copy that refreshes every five minutes. A phone simulator and about 190 automated checks let the team test every menu before customers see it.
Result
Fans are not double-charged, nobody pays without receiving what they bought, and fewer sales are lost to timeouts. New games can be tested on staff phones before they go public.

Direct payments that keep more of each sale

Problem
Most revenue came through USSD airtime, which is shared with the mobile network and the aggregator.
What we built
Fans can now buy on the website and app with CREAM tokens, a debit card or a bank transfer (through Paystack, with OPay being rolled out). USSD stays available for fans without internet.
Result
Every sale that moves from airtime to a direct payment leaves more money with CREAM, because there is no network or aggregator share to pay.

CreamPay: CREAM's own digital bank

Problem
CREAM paid staff, contest winners and artists through outside banks, and paid bank charges on every transfer.
What we built
CreamPay (pay.creamplatform.com), a wallet for creators and businesses. Users send, spend and receive money in naira, US dollars, euros and pounds, with virtual cards and instant transfers. CREAM now runs its own payouts through it: staff salaries, Best of the Streets winners, artist earnings and prize-draw winners.
Result
Most of the bank fees on payouts are gone, money reaches people faster, and winners and artists stay inside the CREAM world, where they can spend their earnings on the platform.

Rewards that make every purchase worth more

Problem
Buying a song was a one-off transaction, with little reason to come back the next day.
What we built
Buying a song now enters the fan into a cash prize draw automatically. The rewards site (rewards.creamplatform.com) adds Koko Draw, Spin D' Koko, and lowest-unique-bid auctions for cash and celebrity items. In those auctions the winner is the lowest bid nobody else placed: if two fans both bid 1, then 1 is no longer unique and 2 becomes the lowest unique bid. Best of the Streets lets talent register, upload a performance video and collect public votes, with a live results screen at the watch-party venue.
Result
Fans have a reason to come back daily, every purchase supports a creator, and CREAM sells more content because of the prizes attached.

A professional way of working

Problem
There was no company code repository and no review step. Changes went straight onto the live server.
What we built
A company GitHub account where every change is reviewed and automatically checked before it goes live, with a message posted to the team's Slack after each release. We also added user analytics (Amplitude), so CREAM can see which features fans use and where purchases drop off.
Result
CREAM owns its code, can bring in new developers safely, and makes product decisions from real usage data instead of guesses.

November 2026 tune-up

Making the website faster on ordinary phones

Most CREAM fans browse on mid-range Android phones over mobile data. We measured the website the way Google does, using Lighthouse, Google's free website-testing tool, with a simulated mid-range phone on a 4G connection. The tests found three things slowing the site down:

  • Oversized photos. Small profile pictures were being downloaded at full camera size. One 36-pixel comment avatar was a 6 MB file.
  • Analytics loading first. The analytics tool, including its session recorder, loaded before the page could respond to taps.
  • Accessibility gaps. The page blocked pinch-to-zoom, and some headings were out of order for screen readers.

We fixed all three. Photos are now resized to the size they are shown at, analytics loads once the page is ready (still recording everything it did before), and zoom and heading order are fixed. We also flagged a few light grey and yellow text colours that are hard to read, for CREAM's designer to review, since those are brand decisions. For fans who pay for every megabyte of mobile data, a lighter page is a cheaper page, and a page that opens sooner is a sale that is less likely to be abandoned.

What this means for you

The same approach works for your business

CREAM's problems are common: a system that breaks under load, software that is risky to change, payment routes that give money away, and fees on moving money. We start by finding where your business loses money or customers, then build only what fixes it, and we measure the result.

AI now writes much of the code. Knowing what to build, and proving it worked, is what we do.
